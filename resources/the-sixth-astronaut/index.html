The Sixth Astronaut

Someone has been cut out of the crew photo. Nobody will say who.

An interactive science-fiction mystery for English learners. You arrive on a space station, read what people say and what the records show, and choose what to do next. Your choices change who you follow, what you find and which ending you reach. It is built to feel like playing a game, but it is a story to read.

  • Level About CEFR B1–B2
  • Ages About 11–15
  • Format Interactive PDF, for screens
  • Endings 10 possible

How the story works

  1. Read. Each page is a short scene. The line at the top gives the day, the time and the place.
  2. Choose. Every decision page ends with two buttons, A and B. Click one and the story goes where you chose.
  3. Notice. What people say is not always what the records show. Logs, messages from Mission Control and spoken claims all look different on the page.
  4. Replay. Use Back or Start again, make a different choice and see what changes. What you find changes what you can prove.

Inside the investigation

Evidence, rival explanations, your decision

The commander smiles. The crew will not meet your eyes. Mission Control says there is no sixth crew member, and there never was. But you saw the pale square in the photo.

Who do you ask? Who do you trust? Different routes lead to different explanations of the same events, so two readers can finish with two very different stories to compare.

  • Four ways to hear the story: what people say, machine logs, records and messages from Mission Control.
  • Two choices on every decision page, and 10 possible endings.
  • A reason to read closely: the next choice depends on the details you noticed.

About B1–B2

Why 154 pages? The story has 29 scenes, and each scene appears again on every route that reaches it. That lets the Back button always return you to the page you really came from. Nobody has to turn to a numbered page.

What it is not. It is a story to read and discuss. It has no answer key, comprehension questions, vocabulary lists, worksheets, lesson plan or teacher guide.

Content note. A serious mystery with some tense moments, and nothing graphic. Please read the opening yourself before using it with younger students.

Ideas for using it

These are suggestions, not part of the PDF.

  • Pairs read together and agree each choice, giving a reason.
  • Students note one clue and who said it, then compare with a partner.
  • After an ending, students go back, change one choice and report what changed.
