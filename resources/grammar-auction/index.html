Grammar Auction

Teams bid fake money on whether sentences are grammatically correct — betting more when confident, risking it all when they're not. Three ready-made sentence sets below, calibrated to real trouble spots, so you can run this without writing a single sentence yourself.

12 min B1–C1 Teen / Adult Group (2–3 teams) Light prep

"Turns error-spotting — usually the most passive grammar task there is — into something with actual stakes."

How to run it: split the class into 2–3 teams (this needs a minimum of about 4 students to work — it's genuinely a team game, not something that scales down to pairs). Give each team a fixed budget, tracked on the board — 100 points is easy math. Read each sentence aloud or display it; teams have 15–20 seconds to confer and write down how much of their remaining budget to bet that it's correct. Reveal the answer: correct bets double, wrong bets are lost entirely. Most points left at the end wins — the real value is in the argument during the betting window, which is where the grammar point actually gets discussed out loud.

Three ready-made sentence sets

Set A — Target: third-person -s and irregular verbs (B1)

CORRECTShe works from home three days a week.

WRONGHe don't like coffee in the morning.

CORRECTThe train leaves at exactly nine o'clock.

WRONGMy brother goes to the gym, but he never goes on weekends actually.

WRONGThe children plays outside every afternoon.

CORRECTIt rarely snows here in December.

WRONGShe have two brothers and one sister.

CORRECTHe always forgets his keys on Mondays.

Set B — Target: past simple vs. present perfect (B2)

CORRECTI have already seen that film twice.

WRONGI have watched that film last night.

CORRECTShe moved to Berlin in 2019.

WRONGWe have finished the project yesterday.

CORRECTHave you ever tried sushi before?

WRONGI never have been to Japan.

CORRECTHe's lived in this city since 2015.

WRONGDid you ever visit Paris? — Yes, I have visited it three years ago.

Set C — Target: conditionals and modals (C1)

CORRECTIf I'd known you were coming, I would have cooked something.

WRONGIf I would have known, I would have told you.

CORRECTShe might have missed the train — she's not usually this late.

WRONGHe must have been at the meeting, but I'm not sure, so maybe he wasn't.

CORRECTHad I realised the risk, I wouldn't have signed the contract.

WRONGIf she will come tomorrow, we can start the meeting on time.

CORRECTYou needn't have brought a gift — but thank you.

WRONGI wish I would have more time this week.

Swap in your own sentences once you've run this a couple of times — by then you'll already know exactly which structures your specific group needs pressure-tested. Needs a minimum of two competing teams; it won't flex down to a 1:1 lesson.
