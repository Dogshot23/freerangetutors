Backstory Objects

Students invent and defend an implausible backstory for a random object already within reach — no advance choosing, no materials. Works identically online and in person.

8 min B1–C1 Teen / Adult Solo, pairs, or groups No prep

"Zero materials, works identically online and in person — 'grab something within arm's reach' is the entire setup."

01Grab an object, right now

Each student grabs any object within arm's reach — a pen, a mug, a phone case, whatever's nearby. No preparation, no choosing in advance. This is what makes it genuinely zero-prep: the object is never planned.

02Invent the backstory

In pairs (or one-on-one with you), each student invents where the object has really been, who owned it before, and how it ended up here right now. The less plausible, the better.

Push for committed, specific detail rather than something vague: not "it's old," but "it belonged to my grandfather's rival in a card game he lost in 1974."
03Cross-examine

The partner asks 2–3 follow-up questions to press for more detail or catch a hole in the story. This is where the real language work happens — not in the initial invention, but in defending it under questioning.

04Share the best one (optional, group only)

If running with a full class, each pair nominates their best backstory to share aloud afterward.

Works as a warm-up, a cooldown, or a genuine filler when a lesson runs short and there's nothing else planned — including a 1:1 online lesson, where "grab something near you" works exactly the same as it would in person.
