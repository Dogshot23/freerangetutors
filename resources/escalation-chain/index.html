Escalation Chain

A short, plain story passes down a chain of retellings — each student adds one deliberate exaggeration before passing it on. Compare the wildly escalated final version to where it started. Real listening pressure, with a built-in payoff.

10 min B1–C1 Teen / Adult Group (4–5+) No prep

"Genuine listening pressure with a built-in payoff — the gap between round one and the final retelling is usually the funniest five minutes of the lesson."

01Start plain

Give the first student a short, boring 3-sentence scenario. Nothing exciting — that's the point, since it's about to become very exciting.

Starting scenario: "I was late for work. My bus didn't come. I had to walk."
02Retell with one exaggeration

That student retells it to the next person in the chain, but must add exactly one exaggerated detail — no more, no less.

e.g. "The bus didn't come because it caught fire" or "the walk was through a sudden hailstorm."
03Pass it on, again and again

The next student retells what they just heard — including the new detail — to the person after them, adding one more exaggeration of their own. Continue through the whole chain.

04Reveal and compare

The last student tells the final version to the whole class. Compare it out loud against the original 3-sentence version — the contrast between "I was late" and whatever it's become is the entire payoff.

In-person only, by design: this needs a genuine chain — one retelling passed along semi-privately, not the whole group hearing every version at once. A synchronous video call collapses that, since everyone hears every retelling simultaneously and the "surprise" of the final reveal disappears. If your platform supports moving students through breakout rooms one at a time, you could adapt it — but that's a specific setup this activity doesn't assume you have.

Needs at least 4–5 students in the chain for the escalation to build up properly — below that, there isn't enough distance between the start and the end for the comparison to land.
