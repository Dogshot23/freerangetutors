Free guide for teachers

Stop the Slop

You’re the boss. AI is the team.

A guide to making and checking teaching resources when AI has had a hand in them. It isn’t trying to persuade you to use AI. If you do, it aims to keep you in charge of the result.

Download the free guide PDF · 63 pages · 18 MB

Free. No sign-up and no email address needed. A5 pages, designed for reading on screen.

What “slop” means here

By slop the guide means output that hasn’t been adequately examined, tested and improved for its intended use. Being made by AI doesn’t define it: you can produce it yourself, and AI-assisted material can be carefully checked.

The method runs from objective to classroom use: start from the objective, choose a format, plan, draft in parts, check, diagnose, repair, re-check, decide, then learn from use. Five decisions stay with you however much AI is involved: the purpose, the task, the level, the accuracy and the final call.

A look inside

Who it’s for

  • Teachers wary of generic, AI-made material who want to know what to look for, and what to do about it.
  • Teachers who use AI and want a way to check and repair what comes back, without giving up the decisions.
  • Teachers who don’t use AI at all but are handed, or find, resources and have to judge them.

How to use it

  • Use the “Where to start” list at the front to jump to the chapter you need.
  • Print the checklist (Appendix A), or the check sheet below, and keep it beside you.
  • Start a mistakes log (Chapter 9) so the same problem doesn’t cost you twice.

What it doesn’t claim

The guide doesn’t promise perfect resources, saved time, classroom success or an AI that can check itself. Its prompts are untested suggestions, and no classroom trial is recorded for any example. The case study is a single small test: it shows what to look for, not how often problems happen.

The Slop Check Sheet

A two-page companion you can print: the quick check, a table that takes a symptom to a likely cause and a controlled repair, and how to decide whether to retain, revise or discard. It condenses chapters 6 to 9 of the guide. It’s a reference, not a validated method.
