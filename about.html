A note on what this site is, how it was made, and why it looks the way it does.

Why this exists

After enough years of teaching, the weight of repetitive admin, mediocre platform tools, and bloated edtech systems becomes genuinely exhausting. A lot of what passes for "teaching technology" adds friction rather than removing it. Lesson plan templates too long to complete in a planning period. Trackers that require more maintenance than the teaching itself. Platforms that cost too much and do too little of what you actually need in a classroom.

Most of it is also designed for the buyer — a department head, an IT coordinator, a procurement decision — not for the teacher who will use it daily. The result is software that performs capability rather than delivering it. Feature lists over usefulness. Dashboards over clarity.

Free Range Tutors is an attempt at something different: a working collection of classroom activities, curated external resources, and things worth finding — made here or found elsewhere, brought together in one place instead of scattered across bookmarks and tabs. Everything included is here because it was genuinely useful for teaching, not because it needed a home. The goal is lower mental fatigue when planning a lesson, not another thing to manage.

How things are designed here

The visual language of this site is deliberately document-like. It does not try to look like a SaaS product. There are no dashboards, no onboarding flows, no progress rings. The interface is meant to recede — to feel closer to a well-organised worksheet than an application.

This is a considered choice. Teachers already carry a significant cognitive load. The tools and resources here should not add to it. A calm, readable layout with clear hierarchy and no unnecessary animation is more useful than a visually impressive one that requires attention to navigate.

The directory itself uses no fixed sidebar, sticky header, or modal-heavy interaction pattern. Everything scrolls as a single document. It behaves predictably on any device, at any screen size, including when projected onto a classroom display mid-lesson.

Low friction
Every resource should be usable immediately, without setup, account creation, or a tutorial. If it needs explaining, it needs simplifying.
Document-like UI
Interfaces should behave like well-designed documents, not applications. Scroll naturally. No fixed chrome. No persistent panels demanding attention.
Reduced cognitive load
Calm colours, legible type, and clear structure. The interface should be forgettable in the best sense — it gets out of the way so the teaching work can happen.
Classroom-first
Practical usefulness in a real lesson, with real students, takes priority over theoretical completeness or visual impressiveness.
Honest about state
Honest about what you're getting: every entry tells you what kind of thing it is and links directly to the destination. Nothing is dressed up as something it isn't.

How to use this site

There is no intended order. Nothing requires registration or installation to browse or open anything here.

The homepage is the whole site: search or filter to find something specific, or just scroll — everything is visible by default, nothing is hidden behind a questionnaire.

Some entries are things built here specifically for teaching English. Others are external sites, tools, or resources found elsewhere and included because they're genuinely worth a teacher's time. Each entry says which kind it is and links straight to it — nothing lives behind an extra step on this site.

If a link stops working or a resource turns out not to be as useful as it looked, feedback is welcome. This is a small project and it goes directly to the person who built it.

This site is built and maintained by one teacher, outside of working hours. It is not a startup, and it is not built around selling things. Most of what is listed here is free to use, because it was put together to solve a teaching problem, not a commercial one. Some linked resources may be paid. Where one is, the entry says so, and you buy it on the seller’s own page, not on this site.

It is, by design, always unfinished. Teaching practice changes. Good resources get found and added. Others turn out not to hold up and get removed. That is the intention.
